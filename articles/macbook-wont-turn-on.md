## Why won't my MacBook turn on?

Before you start, it helps to know what you're actually dealing with. When a Mac won't turn on, the cause almost always falls into one of these buckets:

- **No power reaching the battery** — bad cable, faulty adapter, dead wall outlet, or a lint-clogged port.
- **A drained or deeply discharged battery** — a fully flat battery can need up to a couple of hours on the charger before it shows any life.
- **A stuck power state** — the Mac is technically "on" but frozen, so the screen never lights up.
- **A display issue** — the Mac boots fine but the screen (or its backlight) doesn't, so it looks dead.
- **A software or firmware problem** — a failed macOS update or corrupted startup disk can stop a normal boot.
- **Hardware failure** — the least common, but real (logic board, battery, or screen).

The fixes below walk from the most common and harmless to the most advanced, so you don't do anything drastic before the simple stuff.

## 10 Fixes for a MacBook That Won't Turn On

### Fix 1: Check the power — cable, adapter, and outlet

It sounds too obvious, but a bad power connection is the number-one reason a MacBook won't turn on.

**Step 1:** Plug your Mac into a wall outlet you know works (skip power strips and extension leads for now).
**Step 2:** Use the original Apple charger and cable if you have them. Cheap third-party cables often can't deliver enough wattage.
**Step 3:** On a MagSafe Mac, check the little light on the connector — amber means it's charging, green means full. No light at all points to a power problem.
**Step 4:** Inspect the cable for frays and the ports for dust or lint. Blow out the port gently if it looks clogged.

Give it 10–15 minutes on the charger before deciding it's not working — a flat battery needs time.

[SCREENSHOT 2: Close-up of a MagSafe connector plugged into a MacBook showing the small amber charging LED. ALT: Checking the MagSafe charging light when a MacBook won't turn on]

> **Tip:** Try a different charger or cable if you can borrow one. Swapping the adapter alone fixes a surprising number of "dead" Macs.

### Fix 2: Do a hard force restart

If power is fine but nothing happens, your Mac may be frozen in a stuck state. A force restart clears it.

**Step 1:** Press and hold the **power button** (the Touch ID key on most modern MacBooks) for a full **10 seconds**.
**Step 2:** Release it, wait a few seconds, then press the power button once normally.
**Step 3:** Listen and watch for the startup chime, the fans, or the Apple logo.

This single step revives a huge share of MacBooks that appear dead.

[SCREENSHOT 3: A finger pressing and holding the Touch ID / power button in the top-right corner of a MacBook keyboard. ALT: Force restarting a MacBook that won't turn on by holding the power button for 10 seconds]

### Fix 3: Let a fully drained battery charge (the 3-hour trick)

If your battery ran all the way to zero, the Mac can look completely dead even while charging. This one caught me out personally.

**Step 1:** Connect the official charger and leave everything alone.
**Step 2:** Wait — sometimes it takes anywhere from 15 minutes up to about **3 hours** for a deeply drained battery to hold enough charge to power on.
**Step 3:** After it's had time, try the force restart from Fix 2 again.

> **Heads up:** A deeply discharged battery often shows zero signs of life for the first several minutes. Don't give up in the first two minutes — patience genuinely fixes this.

### Fix 4: Unplug every accessory and external display

A faulty peripheral or an external monitor can stop your Mac from booting normally — or trick you into thinking the screen is dead.

**Step 1:** Disconnect everything: USB drives, hubs, adapters, printers, second monitors, even non-essential cables.
**Step 2:** Leave only the power connection.
**Step 3:** Try turning it on again.

If it boots now, plug accessories back one at a time to find the troublemaker.

### Fix 5: Rule out a display / black screen problem

Sometimes the Mac *is* on — you just can't see it. This is the "macbook won't turn on but is on" situation.

**Step 1:** In a dark room, look very closely at the screen for a faint glow or image (a dim backlight).
**Step 2:** Listen for the startup chime, fan noise, or the keyboard backlight coming on.
**Step 3:** Press **Caps Lock** — if the key's light toggles, your Mac is powered on and it's a display issue, not a power issue.
**Step 4:** Try adjusting brightness (F2) a few times in case it's turned all the way down.

If it's a screen/backlight fault, jump to the black screen section below.

[SCREENSHOT 4: A MacBook in a dim room with the Caps Lock key glowing, showing the Mac is powered on even though the screen is dark. ALT: Testing Caps Lock to check if a MacBook is on when the screen won't turn on]

### Fix 6: Reset the SMC (Intel Macs only)

On older **Intel** MacBooks, the System Management Controller handles power and battery. Resetting it fixes stubborn power issues. (Apple Silicon Macs have no SMC — skip to Fix 7.)

**For Intel MacBooks with a T2 chip:**
**Step 1:** Shut down. Press and hold **Control + Option (left) + Shift (right)** for 7 seconds.
**Step 2:** Keep holding and add the **power button** for another 7 seconds.
**Step 3:** Release all keys, wait a few seconds, then press power to start up.

> **Note:** If you have an Apple Silicon Mac (M1, M2, M3, M4 and newer), there's no SMC to reset — a simple shutdown and restart does the same job.

### Fix 7: Boot into macOS Recovery

If the Mac powers on but can't finish starting up, Recovery Mode lets you repair the disk or reinstall macOS.

**On Apple Silicon (M-series):**
**Step 1:** Fully shut down (hold power 10 seconds if needed).
**Step 2:** Press and hold the **power button** until you see **"Loading startup options."**
**Step 3:** Click **Options → Continue** to enter Recovery.

**On Intel Macs:**
**Step 1:** Turn on and immediately hold **Command (⌘) + R** until the Apple logo appears.

Once in Recovery, open **Disk Utility → First Aid** to repair the startup disk.

[SCREENSHOT 5: The macOS Recovery screen showing Disk Utility, Reinstall macOS, and other options. ALT: Booting a MacBook into macOS Recovery when it won't turn on normally]

### Fix 8: Run First Aid and repair the startup disk

While you're in Recovery, this often fixes a Mac that boots to a black or frozen screen.

**Step 1:** In Recovery, choose **Disk Utility** and click **Continue**.
**Step 2:** Select your main disk (usually "Macintosh HD"), click **First Aid**, then **Run**.
**Step 3:** Let it finish, then restart normally.

### Fix 9: Reinstall macOS (your files stay put)

If the startup disk is fine but macOS is corrupted, reinstalling the system usually keeps your personal files.

**Step 1:** From the Recovery menu, pick **Reinstall macOS**.
**Step 2:** Follow the prompts and choose your startup disk.
**Step 3:** Keep it plugged in and let it run — this can take a while.

> **Safety note:** Reinstalling macOS from Recovery does not erase your files, but if your Mac won't boot at all, back up first using Recovery Mode or a professional if the data matters.

### Fix 10: Revive with DFU mode, then call Apple

If nothing above works, the deepest option is **DFU mode**, which revives the firmware using a second Mac. It's the last stop before a repair.

**Step 1:** On a second Mac, open **Apple Configurator** (or Finder on newer macOS) and connect the two with a USB-C cable — use the **back-left USB-C port** on the dead Mac.
**Step 2:** Put the dead Mac into DFU mode (the exact key combo depends on the model — follow Apple's official steps).
**Step 3:** Choose **Revive** first (keeps data); use **Restore** only as a last resort (erases everything).

If DFU fails, it's time for [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/mac) or an authorized service provider — you're likely looking at a battery, screen, or logic-board issue.

## MacBook won't turn on: black screen

If the Mac powers on (fans, chime, or Caps Lock light) but the **screen stays black**, it's a display problem, not a power one.

- Turn brightness up with **F2** — it may be at zero.
- Force restart (Fix 2) to clear a stuck display state.
- Disconnect external monitors that may be "stealing" the display output.
- Boot into **Safe Mode** (Apple Silicon: hold power → select disk → hold **Shift** → "Continue in Safe Mode") to rule out software.
- If you see a faint image, the backlight may have failed — that's a hardware repair.

[SCREENSHOT 7: A MacBook Pro showing a very faint desktop image on an otherwise black screen, indicating a backlight problem. ALT: MacBook won't turn on black screen showing a faint image from a failed backlight]

## MacBook won't turn on or charge

If there's **no charging light and no power**, focus on the power chain:

- Test a different **outlet, cable, and adapter** — one at a time.
- Clean the charging port (lint blocks MagSafe and USB-C more than you'd think).
- On MagSafe, no light usually means the cable, adapter, or port is the problem.
- Let it sit on the charger for the full 3 hours (Fix 3) before assuming the battery is dead.
- Still nothing? The battery or charging circuit may need service.

## MacBook Air won't turn on

The steps are the same as MacBook Pro, with a couple of Air-specific notes:

- The MacBook Air's power button is the **Touch ID key** in the top-right corner — hold it 10 seconds for a force restart.
- Fanless Airs run silent, so use the **Caps Lock test** (Fix 5) to check if it's actually on.
- Give a drained Air extra charging time; the smaller battery can read empty for a while.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why won't my MacBook turn on even when it's plugged in?
Usually the battery is deeply drained, the charger or cable is faulty, or the charging port is blocked. Try a different outlet and adapter, clean the port, and leave it charging for up to 3 hours before trying a force restart.

### My MacBook won't turn on but the screen is black — is it broken?
Not necessarily. Press Caps Lock or listen for fans and the chime. If there are signs of life, your Mac is on and it's a display or backlight problem, not a dead machine. Turn up brightness and force restart first.

### How do I force restart a MacBook that won't turn on?
Press and hold the power button (the Touch ID key on modern MacBooks) for a full 10 seconds, release, wait a moment, then press it again. This clears a frozen power state and revives most unresponsive Macs.

### How long should I charge a completely dead MacBook?
Leave it on the official charger for at least 15 minutes, and be prepared to wait up to about 3 hours for a fully drained battery. A deeply discharged battery can show zero signs of life at first, so don't give up early.

### How do I boot my MacBook into Recovery Mode if it won't start normally?
On Apple Silicon Macs, shut down, then press and hold the power button until you see "Loading startup options," then choose Options. On Intel Macs, turn on and immediately hold Command + R until the Apple logo appears.

### My MacBook won't turn on after a macOS update — what now?
A failed update can leave the startup disk corrupted. Boot into Recovery Mode, run Disk Utility → First Aid, and if that doesn't help, reinstall macOS from Recovery (your files usually stay intact).

### Do I need to reset the SMC on my M1/M2 MacBook?
No. Apple Silicon Macs (M1, M2, M3, M4 and newer) don't have an SMC. Simply shutting down fully and restarting does the same thing. SMC resets only apply to older Intel Macs.

### When should I take my MacBook to Apple?
If you've tried the power checks, force restart, long charge, Recovery Mode, and DFU revive with no luck, you're likely facing a battery, screen, or logic-board fault. That's the point to contact Apple Support or an authorized service provider.
